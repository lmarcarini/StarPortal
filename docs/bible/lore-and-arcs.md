# Lore and Arcs

## The deep history

The Ring network was never built for travel. It is one vast instrument, made by the Anteri to open a single door out of the universe; travel between worlds was a side effect.

This is the canon truth. The player only ever sees pieces of it, through sources that each get part of it wrong.

| When | What really happened |
| --- | --- |
| \~200,000 years ago | The Anteri build the network across three domains. Its Rings work together as a lens aimed at one point outside spacetime, the Far Side. |
| \~40,000 years ago | **The Departure.** The Anteri dial the tenth seal and cross. The crossing does not take all of them whole. Fragments of their minds stay caught in the network and become the Murmur. They take the Keystone with them; the network stays on. |
| \~38,000 years ago | The Ossar, the Anteri's Ring-keepers, are left with machines they maintain but do not understand. Their maintenance rites harden into a religion, the Hierarchate. They call the Murmur "the Hungry Silence" and teach that unsanctioned dialing feeds it. They are partly right. |
| \~12,000 years ago | The Halvane, a younger species, begin to study the network and become its scholars. They fight a long war with the Ossar. |
| \~6,000 years ago | The Halvane discover the ninth seal and flee to another domain, hiding its glyph. Their life-extension treatment has made them sterile; they are slowly dying out. |
| \~5,000 years ago | The Ossar take humans from Earth as labour. The Kindred descend from them. After a Murmur outbreak, the Ossar bury Earth's Ring and abandon the planet. |
| 1958 | Prospectors find the Ring in the Gallow salt deposit. |
| 1961 | **The Gallow Incident.** A team dials an address copied from the Ring's own inscriptions. Something comes through, 14 people die, and the program is buried. Continuity is created to keep it buried. |
| Present day | After a budget fight, the Directorate reopens the program as the Far Side Protocol, named after words in the 1961 file, and appoints the player as Director. |

What came through in 1961 was an Ossar acolyte touched by the Murmur. Continuity has kept him alive in a hidden facility ever since. He is the root of most government lies and matters again in Arc 6.

## Factions and their arcs

Ten factions shape the campaign; each wants something specific, offers something specific, and has its own arc that runs beside the main one.

| Faction | Wants | Offers | Right about |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Directorate | Results without scandal | Staff, soldiers, legitimacy | Secrecy has kept people alive |
| The Committee | Value, credit, control | The budget | The program answers to someone |
| Continuity | Control of alien technology; 1961 kept buried | Dossiers, black funds | Some truths cause panic |
| Halcyon Dynamics | Contracts, then the program itself | Money, materials, production | Hoarded technology helps nobody |
| The Ossar | Obedience and tolls on the network | Truces, captured records | Reckless dialing feeds the Murmur |
| The Halvane | An heir for their Archive | Gifts, sky charts, deep technology | Humanity is not ready yet |
| The Kindred | Freedom from the Ossar | Trade, volunteers, oral histories | Earth abandoned them once |
| The Murmur | To come home | Visions, impossible clues | The Anteri left part of themselves behind |
| The Dravian Federation | A Ring of its own, or yours | Back-channel deals, defectors, intelligence on Continuity | No single nation should hold the network |
| The Meridian Accord | A share of the program and a say in it | Funding, scientists, diplomatic cover | The Ring is humanity's problem, not one country's |

### The Directorate: "Mandate"

The player starts on probation. Early results earn trust; the Continuity audit tests it. The arc ends with the player either confirmed as Director or placed under a Continuity appointee, with reduced powers, for the rest of Arc 2.

### The Committee: "The Hearings"

Three senators hold the votes. Chair Ruth Adeyemi is a pragmatist, Sen. Graham Vosk a hawk who wants weapons, Sen. Elena Marquez a reformer who wants disclosure. Each quarter shifts the majority. Their balance decides whether disclosure in Arc 5 is a vote or a leak.

### Continuity: "The Gallow File"

Deputy Director Simon Ashby runs it. Its dossiers are mostly true, with key lies. The player finds a doctored dossier that hides what a world really is, then the 1961 file, then the hidden acolyte. Continuity tries to take the base (Arc 2 setback). It ends exposed, absorbed, or quietly allied.

### Halcyon Dynamics: "Private Sector"

CEO Victoria Lund buys declassified technology and wants more. Halcyon goes from contractor to partner to rival. In the late game it builds its own dialing device from the player's own technology. Its reckless dialing draws the Murmur toward Earth.

### The Ossar: "The Toll"

The Hierarchate taxes travel and punishes unsanctioned dialing. Incursions escalate until a heretic sect, the Unbound, led by the disgraced keeper Tavor, contacts the player. The arc ends in a broken Hierarchate, a reformed one, or open war.

### The Halvane: "Inheritance"

Archivist Seren tests the player with gifts that carry conditions. The Halvane withhold the ninth seal until trust is earned. Their arc ends with a choice: inherit the Archive and its burden, or let the Halvane end on their own terms.

### The Kindred: "Council of Hearths"

Scattered worlds trade with the player and ask for help against the Ossar. The player's support decides whether they unite into a Council or fracture into rival worlds. A united Council fights beside the player in Arc 7.

### The Murmur: "Homecoming"

It begins as telemetry noise and staff with shared dreams. It grows into contamination and impossible clues. The player can study it, fight it, or listen. What the player learns decides which endings are open.

### The Dravian Federation: "Cold Front"

A rival power that learns of the program early. Its agents slip in among recruits in Arc 2, and it copies declassified technology in Arc 3. In Arcs 4 and 5 it recovers an Ossar dialing relic and starts making unsanctioned dials of its own. The arc ends in a shared program, a standoff held by deterrence, or sabotage that the player must answer for.

### The Meridian Accord: "Partners"

Allied nations who start with quiet briefings and favours. After Daylight, Accord liaisons arrive at CAIRN, bringing money, scientists and a vote on how the Ring is used. The arc ends in a true international program or a split that leaves the player more independent and less protected.

## Campaign arcs

Each arc asks one question, changes at least one rule, and most take something away for a while; the setback is the arc's signature, not a punishment.

| Arc | Question | Gameplay added or changed | Setback | Ends when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prologue: Cold Ring | Can the Ring be dialed safely? | Tutorial: the 1958 field note and carving rubbing, the dial console, power, dialing, the MOTH | None | The first Threshold team returns |
| 1: First Light | Who is out there? | Expeditions, research, Kindred contact, the first incursion | No Shutter yet: every incoming wormhole is dangerous | The Shutter is online and five worlds are surveyed |
| 2: The Gallow File | Who is lying? | Source grades, plants among staff, lie detection, Halcyon contracts | The Continuity audit freezes the official budget; the base runs on black funds and Halcyon money | Continuity is exposed, absorbed or bargained with |
| 3: The Toll | What does the network cost? | Outposts, defence, request ripples, the Unbound | The Ossar seize a Kindred world that supplies anterite; the Shutter can now be breached | The Ossar push is broken, or a truce holds |
| 4: Ninth Seal | Where did the Halvane go? | The domain glyph, 9-seal dialing, large power projects | The first 9-seal dial blacks out a region, and Exposure spikes | A team reaches the Halvane Refuge |
| 5: Daylight | What happens when the world knows? | Disclosure: Exposure becomes public opinion; Meridian Accord partners and a bigger budget; Dravian pressure | If disclosure is a leak, not a vote, a month of hearings restricts dialing | A new public mandate is in place |
| 6: The Murmur | What lives in the network? | Quarantine rules, contaminated staff, limits on dialing; puzzles solved from old logs and Ring memory | Ring lockdown for most of the arc | The Keystone pattern is recovered |
| 7: Tenth Seal | What was the Ring for? | Keystone crafting, the 10-seal power project, a faction coalition | A final incursion during the dial | The ending |

## Endings

The ending is chosen at the Tenth Seal, but what the player has learned and who stands with them decide which choices are available.

| Ending | The choice | Requires | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reunion | Open the door fully and let the Murmur cross | Halvane trust and a deep understanding of the Murmur | The Murmur goes home and the network goes dark forever. Earth is alone again, but changed. |
| Seal | Destroy the Keystone | Always available | The network survives and humanity inherits it. The Murmur stays, contained but not gone. |
| Claim | Use the open door as a weapon and power source | Continuity or Halcyon in control at the end | Earth becomes a power in the network, and the cost shows in the epilogue |
| Crossing | The Director goes through | Most of the hidden lore found (Codex threshold) | The secret ending: the player sees the Far Side |

After the ending, an epilogue gives each faction its own outcome based on its state. Play can then continue in the same save, except after Reunion, where the Ring is dead and the game offers a new start instead.

## Lore delivery

The lore has three layers, and each player goes as deep as they want; the surface layer alone tells a complete story.

| Layer | Who sees it | Delivered by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Surface | Everyone | Arc events, briefings, radio calls, cutscenes |
| Record | Curious players | Codex entries, full dossiers in the Archive, artefact notes, Kindred oral histories, drone-feed scenes |
| Truth | Dedicated players | Contradictions between sources, glyph names and their meanings, Anteri inscriptions, the hidden address rule |

- **The Codex** unlocks entries from sources. When sources disagree, an entry is marked contested and shows each version side by side: the government's account beside what the player found.
- **Glyph names** come from Anteri words. Inscriptions offer an optional decipherment puzzle that reveals what each glyph means.
- **Writing rules:** no info dumps; Codex entries of 150 words or fewer; every faction is right about something; nothing in the Record contradicts the canon except on purpose, through a named liar.
- **The canon file.** A lore canon document in the repository holds the true timeline and every planted lie. It is the reference for all writing.
