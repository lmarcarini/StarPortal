# The First Four Puzzles

The Prologue's four address puzzles: the documents the player reads, the images they see, how
each puzzle is solved, the hints, the answer, the world behind it and that world's position.

Bible: [gate-and-address-spec.md#where-addresses-come-from](../bible/gate-and-address-spec.md#where-addresses-come-from),
[#the-opening-puzzle](../bible/gate-and-address-spec.md#the-opening-puzzle),
[#puzzle-design-rules](../bible/gate-and-address-spec.md#puzzle-design-rules),
[lore-and-arcs.md](../bible/lore-and-arcs.md) (Prologue: "Can the Ring be dialed safely?"),
[overview.md](../bible/overview.md), [art-and-audio.md](../bible/art-and-audio.md).
Status: draft for the owner's approval. The owner approved the names of LINTEL, MILESTONE,
STILLWATER and the people around them; the names new in this revision are marked in the list at
the end.

**To solve cold:** read only the "Document" and "Image" sections of a puzzle, try it, then read
its "Solution". The answers appear nowhere above a puzzle's Solution section.

## At a glance

| # | Delivered | Holds | Trick |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | New game | Commission order, sketch | A tutorial: the order describes each carving plainly and gives the order; match each to the palette, enter seven, leave the eighth to the console, dial. It also orders the capacitor bank installed first |
| 2 | First lock of world 1 | Field note, rubbing | The note gives the order but describes the carvings loosely or by marks only the rubbing shows; the rubbing shows the carvings but not their order |
| 3 | First lock of world 2 | Letter, two horizon-camera frames, horizon record | A ring of carvings: find where it starts and which way it runs, and put back the piece that fell |
| 4 | First lock of world 3 | Field note, song transcript | Seven sung actions name seven objects; two nights disagree on the order, so dial to settle |

The four share one thread. In 1958 the survey found carved stones by the Ring, a sill at its foot
and a lintel over the way into its chamber, and guessed that someone who came after the builders
left them as signs. In 1961 a commission ordered the sill's address dialed first; the order was
never carried out. Puzzle 1 carries it out. Puzzle 2 reads the lintel. Puzzle 3 finds the same
hand's sign on the far side. Puzzle 4 comes from a man who never saw the lintel and sang its kind
of address in his sleep.

**Delivery in M1.5.** There are no probes until M3, so every document in this file arrives on
a lock or at the start: puzzle 1's documents at the start; puzzle 2's documents and world 1's
dossier on world 1's first lock; puzzle 3's documents and world 2's dossier on world 2's first
lock; puzzle 4's documents and world 3's dossier on world 3's first lock; world 4's dossier on
world 4's first lock. In the fiction, the Gate Room's first-lock routine pushes a camera and a
sampling line through the horizon on a pole (the "horizon record"); that is where the far-side
frames and the dossiers' readings come from. The pole camera is a permanent fixture of the Gate
Room and makes a horizon record on every first lock. When probes arrive in M3 (the MOTH first),
they add to it; they do not replace it.

## Puzzle 1: The Sill Stone

**Delivered:** at the start of a new game, in the Archive, as the only two items there. The Gate
Room's dial console is open beside them. The base has no capacitors yet, so the console cannot
dial until the bank is installed from the Gate Room's capacitor gauge (6 in-game hours, card #94).

**Teaches:** install the capacitor bank; find each carving on the console's palette; drag the
glyphs into the slots in order; leave seal 8 to the console; dial. Nothing is deduced: the order
hands over the address, and the player performs each step once.

### Document 1A: Commission Order No. 1

- **Kind:** note
- **Title:** Gallow Commission, Order No. 1
- **Source:** the 1961 Gallow Commission, signed by its secretary, Mr. C. Dunmore; a Records
  Section slip is clipped to the top

> RECORDS SECTION. RELEASE SLIP.
> Released to the Director on the reopening of the site, under Standing Order 61/4. The bank
> fitted in 1961 was removed when the works were closed. The order stands.
>
> ---
>
> GALLOW COMMISSION. ORDER No. 1.
> 6 March 1961. For the officer in charge at Gallow.
>
> 1. No attempt is to be made on the Ring until the capacitor bank is installed in the Gate Room
> and holds its charge. Installation is six hours' work.
>
> 2. The first attempt shall be the proving address: the carvings cut on the sill stone at the
> foot of the Ring, recorded by the 1958 survey and drawn by Mr. Pell (attached). Enter them in
> this order and in no other:
>
> First, the line that climbs in square steps.
> Second, the two pairs of pointed wings either side of a short body, the upper pair the larger.
> Third, the thin triangle lying flat, its square corner at the lower right.
> Fourth, the tall pole with a triangle standing out from its upper part.
> Fifth, the upright with two short arms, one each side, bent upward.
> Sixth, the tall frame with a slab swung half open inside it and a dot near the slab's edge.
> Seventh, the trunk that splits and splits again as it climbs.
>
> 3. The eighth carving, the round-bellied pot with a spout, is this Ring's own mark. The
> console sets it at every attempt. It is not to be entered.
>
> 4. The result is to be reported to this office before any second attempt.
>
> C. Dunmore, Secretary to the Commission.

**How it looks:** a typed foolscap sheet, the release slip a smaller, newer slip held on with a
paper clip. Across the foot of the order, at an angle, a red rubber stamp: **NOT ACTIONED**, with
a date, 11 March 1961, and illegible initials.

### Image 1B: The sill stone

- **Kind:** image
- **Title:** Sill stone, from above
- **Source:** T. Pell, pencil on squared paper, 1958, attached to Order No. 1

**What it shows.** A low, flat slab drawn from above in firm pencil, about twice as wide as it is
deep, with the squares of the paper showing through. Eight carvings sit on its face in two rows
of four, each drawn as its palette picture, clean and whole. Nothing on the sheet is damaged,
numbered or arrowed.

| | Column 1 | Column 2 | Column 3 | Column 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Top row | Stairs | Wedge | Cactus | Tree |
| Bottom row | Butterfly | Flag | Doorway | Kettle |

Along the bottom edge, in Pell's hand: *Sill stone, Gallow, from above. T.P.*

### Solution

1. Install the capacitor bank from the Gate Room's gauge, as item 1 orders. The console cannot
   dial until it is in and charged.
2. Match each line of item 2 to a carving on the sketch and on the palette, in the order's order:
   - First, the line that climbs in square steps: the **Stairs**.
   - Second, two pairs of pointed wings, the upper pair larger: the **Butterfly**.
   - Third, the thin triangle lying flat with its square corner at lower right: the **Wedge**.
   - Fourth, the tall pole with a triangle from its upper part: the **Flag**.
   - Fifth, the upright with two short arms bent upward: the **Cactus**.
   - Sixth, the frame with a slab half open and a dot: the **Doorway**.
   - Seventh, the trunk that splits again and again: the **Tree**.
3. The eighth carving, the pot with the spout, is already in seal 8 (CAIRN's mark, the Kettle).
   The player does not enter it.
4. The player dials. The full bank is enough, and the Ring locks.

The one "read it carefully" moment is the sketch's layout: the order runs down each column in
turn, so a player who copies the drawing row by row gets a different sequence (Stairs, Wedge,
Cactus, Tree, Butterfly, Flag, Doorway), which no world has. The order says to enter the carvings
in its own order, "and in no other".

Expected time for a first-time player: one to three minutes of reading and dragging, plus the
six in-game hours of installation (which the player can speed through).

### How the carvings were checked

Each description in Order No. 1 was compared with all 36 picture descriptions in
`localization/glyphs.csv`. For each, the table names the glyphs that come closest and the words
in the description that rule them out. No description fits more than one glyph.

| Order's words | Glyph | Closest others, and what rules them out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A line that climbs in square steps | Stairs | Ladder (two rails and crossbars, no steps), Mountain (a jagged line to two peaks, not square), Arrow (one straight slanting line) |
| Two pairs of pointed wings either side of a short body, the upper pair larger | Butterfly | Spider (four limbs bent like knees, not pairs of wings), Kite (one diamond), Heron (a bird, no paired wings) |
| A thin triangle lying flat, square corner at the lower right | Wedge | Tent (a tall upright triangle with a line from its peak), Flag (a triangle on a pole), Hourglass (two triangles), Boomerang (two arms at a wide bend) |
| A tall pole with a triangle standing out from its upper part | Flag | Sailboat (two triangles over a boat), Arrow (a V at the end of a slanting line), Anchor (a crossbar, no triangle), Kite (a diamond) |
| An upright with two short arms, one each side, bent upward | Cactus | Anchor (a crossbar near the top and long arms sweeping from the foot), Hand (five lines and a stem), Fork (three prongs on a curved base), Tree (a trunk that splits again and again) |
| A tall frame with a slab swung half open inside it and a dot near the slab's edge | Doorway | Chair (a back and seat on two supports, no frame), Bridge (two posts and a sagging line) |
| A trunk that splits and splits again as it climbs | Tree | Fork (three prongs on one base, splitting once), Hand (five lines from one point), Cactus (two arms, no further splitting) |
| A round-bellied pot with a spout | Kettle | Goblet (an open top on a stem), Bell (a dome with a dot inside), Candle (a narrow block) |

The sketch shows each carving as its palette picture too, so a player who trusts the drawing over
the words reaches the same glyphs.

### Where players get stuck, and the analyst's hints

| Stuck on | Gentle hint | Direct hint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dial disabled: no capacitors | "Order No. 1, item 1: nothing before the capacitor bank is in." | "Install the capacitor bank from the gauge in the Gate Room. It takes six hours." |
| Matching a line of the order to a carving | "Each line of item 2 describes one carving on Pell's sketch." | "Find the carving the line describes on the sketch, then the same picture on the palette." |
| Copying the sketch row by row | "The order gives its own sequence: 'in this order and in no other'." | "Follow item 2 line by line: Stairs, then the Butterfly, then the Wedge." |
| Trying to enter the pot with the spout | "Item 3 says what to do with the eighth carving." | "The pot is this Ring's own mark. It is already in seal 8; enter only seven." |

### Answer

**Seals 1 to 7:** Stairs, Butterfly, Wedge, Flag, Cactus, Doorway, Tree (glyphs 35, 24, 29, 10,
19, 22, 8). Seal 8: CAIRN's mark (Kettle, glyph 2).

**World: SILL.** A dark, airless world of red rock under a dim sun, and the Prologue's first
answer: the Ring can be dialed, and nothing comes back. **Safe:** yes.

**World dossier (arrives on SILL's first lock):**

> WORLD DOSSIER: SILL (PRELIMINARY)
> Source of address: the sill stone at the foot of the Ring (Gallow Commission, Order No. 1;
> Pell drawing, 1958).
> Compiled from the horizon record of the first lock.
> Status: Dead. Safe.
>
> The far Ring stands on a shelf of dark red rock at the foot of a cliff. The sky is a deep
> violet even in daylight, and a dim orange sun sits low over the cliff's edge. The sampling line
> drew almost nothing: a trace of nitrogen at a pressure too low to measure well. Temperature at
> the horizon is about minus twenty degrees. There is no water, no wind and no life of any kind
> on the camera's frames.
>
> A flat slab is set in the rock before the far Ring, where the sill stone lies before ours. Its
> face is bare. The dust on it is unmarked.
>
> The Ring was dialed, held its lock, and nothing came through from the far side. The
> Committee's first question has its answer. The Ring can be dialed safely, at least to here.
>
> Analyst's note: the order that named this address was signed in March 1961 and stamped as not
> carried out five days later. Whatever was dialed at Gallow that year, it was not this.

**Position:** (-2065, 2433, 1107) light years, 3,959 ly from CAIRN. Seal 1's anchor (35) is
2,221.6 ly from CAIRN, so the lock needs 366.6 MWh, inside one 400 MWh bank.

## Puzzle 2: The Lintel

**Delivered:** on SILL's first lock, Folder 1 of the Object 58 file arrives in the Archive, with a
notification on the wormhole screen. It holds documents 2A and 2B and a Records Section slip:
"Released to the Director on the first confirmed lock, under Standing Order 61/4. Folder 2 is held
until the second."

**Teaches:** read two things together; a description can name a carving by a mark that only the
other source shows.

### Document 2A: Field Note 14

- **Kind:** note
- **Title:** Gallow Survey, Field Note 14
- **Source:** Dr. A. Ellery, leader of the 1958 survey, typed for the file

> GALLOW SURVEY. FIELD NOTE 14.
> 9 June 1958. A. Ellery.
>
> The lintel came up this morning from the passage north of the chamber, in four pieces. Seven
> carvings run along its face. Mr. Pell took a rubbing in the shed from the pieces as they lay
> on the bench, so his sheet does not follow the stone. My sketch from the passage was spoiled by
> the damp. What survives is my list, which keeps the order of the stone.
>
> The carvings were touched, always in one direction. The polish of hands is heaviest on the
> first and runs on along the face. In that order:
>
> First, the climbing mark.
>
> Second, the one Mr. Pell calls the surrendering man. I take it for a plant of dry country.
>
> Third, the one the crack runs through.
>
> Fourth, what holds a ship in place. Mr. Pell served in the Navy and says nothing else would be
> cut so.
>
> Fifth, the one with a point broken off.
>
> Sixth, the ship.
>
> Seventh and last, what opens a locked door.
>
> Each of the seven is also cut among the marks on the Ring itself. I do not think that is
> chance. I would like to know what they say before anyone tries them.

### Image 2B: The rubbing

- **Kind:** image
- **Title:** Lintel rubbing, as laid
- **Source:** T. Pell, graphite on tracing paper, 9 June 1958

**What it shows.** A long, slightly creased sheet, about four times as wide as it is tall, grey
graphite on off-white paper with the grain of the stone showing through. Four pale shapes with
ragged edges mark the four pieces of the lintel as they lay on the bench, left to right, with
gaps of bare paper between them. Seven carvings sit inside them, each drawn as its palette
picture, clean and whole except for the two marks below:

| Piece | Carvings, left to right | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Key, Crown | The tip of the Crown's **middle point is broken off**: a short flat stub with a rough edge. No other carving on the sheet is chipped. |
| B | Sailboat | None |
| C | Stairs, Flag | A thin dark **crack** starts at the top edge of piece C, runs down through the Flag (crossing its pole just below the triangle) and dies out before the bottom edge. The Stairs beside it are untouched. It is the only crack inside a piece. |
| D | Anchor, Cactus | None |

The two marks must be easy to see at the default zoom: the crack is darker than the grain, and
the broken point is plainly shorter than its neighbours.

Under each carving Pell has pencilled a small number, 1 to 7, left to right (Key is 1, Cactus
is 7). In the lower right corner, in the same pencil: *Lintel, shed, as laid. T.P.*

### Solution

1. The note keeps the order of the stone; the rubbing does not (Pell's numbers are the bench).
2. The note alone cannot be dialed. "The climbing mark" could be the Stairs, the Ladder, the
   Mountain or the Arrow on the palette; "the surrendering man" could be the Hand, the Cactus or
   the Tree; "what opens a locked door" could be the Key or the Doorway; and the third and fifth
   entries name no shape at all, only damage.
3. The rubbing settles each one, because only seven carvings are on it:
   - First, the climbing mark: the only carving on the sheet that climbs is the **Stairs**.
   - Second, the surrendering man or dry-country plant: an upright with two arms raised, the
     **Cactus**.
   - Third, the one the crack runs through: the **Flag** in piece C.
   - Fourth, what holds a ship in place: the **Anchor**.
   - Fifth, the one with a point broken off: the **Crown** in piece A.
   - Sixth, the ship: the **Sailboat**.
   - Seventh, what opens a locked door: the **Key**.
4. The player finds each on the palette and drags it to the next slot. Seal 8 is already
   CAIRN's mark. The charge is 366.6 MWh, inside the 400 MWh bank. The player dials and locks.

Expected time for a first-time player: three to five minutes.

### Where players get stuck, and the analyst's hints

Hints go from gentle to direct; the difficulty setting decides how soon the next one is offered.

| Stuck on | Gentle hint | Direct hint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trying the note alone | "Dr. Ellery's list gives the order, but some of it only makes sense with the stone in front of you. Pell's rubbing is the stone." | "Read each line of the note against the seven carvings on the rubbing. Only one carving on the sheet fits each line." |
| Dialing in Pell's numbered order | "Pell numbered the pieces as they lay on the bench. Ellery says her list keeps the order of the stone." | "Use the note's order. Its first carving is the climbing mark." |
| "The one the crack runs through" | "Look at the rubbing for damage, not shape." | "A crack runs down through piece C. The carving it crosses is the Flag." |
| "The one with a point broken off" | "Which carving on the rubbing has points, and is one of them shorter than the others?" | "The Crown in piece A has lost the tip of its middle point." |
| "The surrendering man" (reaching for the Anchor, whose arms also sweep up) | "Ellery and Pell disagree on what it is, but both see something standing upright with arms raised. The ship's fittings come later in the list." | "The Anchor is the fourth carving. The second is the Cactus." |
| Not finding a carving on the console | "Every carving on the rubbing is also on the Ring, so it is on the palette." | "The palette shows each carving clean; match the shape and ignore the crack and the chip." |

### Answer

**Seals 1 to 7:** Stairs, Cactus, Flag, Anchor, Crown, Sailboat, Key (glyphs 35, 19, 10, 15,
5, 6, 4). Seal 8: CAIRN's mark (Kettle, glyph 2).

**World: LINTEL.** A dead world of grey grit under a black sky, where someone who came after the
builders left a stone facing the Ring. **Safe:** yes.

**World dossier (arrives on LINTEL's first lock):**

> WORLD DOSSIER: LINTEL (PRELIMINARY)
> Source of address: Gallow lintel, 1958 (Field Note 14, Pell rubbing).
> Compiled from the horizon record of the first lock.
> Status: Dead. Safe.
>
> The far Ring stands on a low platform in a plain of grey grit under a black sky. The sampling
> line drew a trace of carbon dioxide and argon, far too thin to breathe. Temperature at the
> horizon is about minus seventy degrees. Radiation is raised but within suit limits for short
> visits. There is no water, no wind and no life of any kind on the camera's frames.
>
> Thirty metres in front of the Ring stands a disc of worked stone, set on edge to face it. See
> the horizon record and frames 1 and 2.
>
> The Ring was dialed, held its lock and, as at SILL, nothing came through from the far side.
> Two locks, two quiet worlds.
>
> Analyst's note: the disc is cut from the local rock with metal tools. Its tool marks are worn
> but clear; the Ring beside it shows no tool marks at all. The Gallow lintel was also cut long
> after the Ring it stood beside. Whoever cut both came after the builders, and came through
> here on the way to us, or from us.

**Position:** (-68, -3864, -1500) light years, 3,234 ly from CAIRN. Seal 1's anchor (35) is
2,221.6 ly from CAIRN, so the lock needs 366.6 MWh.

## Puzzle 3: The Milestone

**Delivered:** on LINTEL's first lock, all four documents arrive in the Archive together, with
a notification on the wormhole screen: Folder 2 of the Object 58 file (3A) and the lock's
horizon record (3B, 3C, 3D).

**Teaches:** the far side holds clues; a puzzle can say where its missing piece is.

### Document 3A: Object 58, Folder 2

- **Kind:** dossier
- **Title:** Object 58, Folder 2: Correspondence, 1959
- **Source:** Directorate Records Section; a letter from Dr. A. Ellery to the Directorate liaison

> RECORDS SECTION. RELEASE SLIP.
> Released to the Director on the second confirmed lock, under Standing Order 61/4.
> Folder 3 is held until the third.
>
> ---
>
> Gallow, 3 February 1959.
> To Mr. C. Dunmore, Directorate liaison.
>
> Dear Mr. Dunmore,
>
> You asked for my view of the lintel in plain words, for your committee. Here it is.
>
> The Ring is not of our world, and I will not pretend to know how it was made. The lintel is
> another matter. It is ordinary stone from this valley, cut with hard metal tools, and it is a
> great deal younger than the Ring. Someone came after the builders, used the Ring, and left a
> sign over the door for whoever came next. A milestone, if you like.
>
> A people who leave one milestone leave others. If the Ring is ever opened, I ask that someone
> look near the far Ring for worked stone carrying seven marks.
>
> How to read such a stone, if it is found. On ours the polish began on the climbing mark, the
> one that rises in square notches, and ran along the face in the direction the notches climb.
> I believe that was their rule and not an accident of where the stone was set. Begin with the
> climbing mark. Go the way it climbs.
>
> If the stone is broken I can only say what we learned in the shed: the pieces are never far.
> Look on the ground beneath it.
>
> I enclose nothing further. My notes on Mr. Haskett I am keeping back until I understand them
> better, which may be never.
>
> Yours sincerely,
> A. Ellery

### Image 3B: Horizon camera, frame 1

- **Kind:** image
- **Title:** LINTEL, first lock, horizon camera frame 1
- **Source:** the Gate Room's pole camera, pushed through the horizon

**What it shows.** A grainy black-and-white still, slightly vignetted, with a frame number and
time stamped in the lower corner. Grey grit, black sky. In the middle of the frame, seen
straight on from about head height and thirty metres away, a disc of pale grey stone about
1.5 m across, standing on edge in a low socket of the same stone. A round boss sits at its
centre. Seven recesses are cut evenly around the rim, about 51 degrees apart, each holding one
carving cut **upright** (every carving stands the right way up for the viewer, whatever its
place on the rim).

| Place on the rim | Carving |
| --- | --- |
| Bottom (6 o'clock) | Stairs |
| Lower right (a little past 4 o'clock) | Doorway |
| Right, above level (about half past 2) | Cactus |
| Top, just right of centre (about 1 o'clock) | Ladder |
| Top, just left of centre (about 11 o'clock) | Butterfly |
| Left, above level (about half past 9) | Wedge |
| Lower left (a little before 8 o'clock) | **Empty recess:** a clean-edged hollow with a rough floor, shaped like a tall rectangle |

Below the empty recess, at the foot of the disc, a small pale shape lies on the grit, too small
to read at this distance.

### Image 3C: Horizon camera, frame 2

- **Kind:** image
- **Title:** LINTEL, first lock, horizon camera frame 2 (long lens)
- **Source:** the same camera, long lens, aimed at the foot of the disc

**What it shows.** The same grain and stamp. The bottom left of the disc fills the top of the
frame, the empty recess just visible. Below it on the grit lies a broken tablet of the same
pale stone, roughly a tall rectangle with one corner snapped off, face up. It holds one carving,
**upside down** as it lies: the Candle rotated 180 degrees, so its narrow block is at the top
and the short line runs down from the block's middle to end in the dot.

### Document 3D: Horizon record

- **Kind:** log
- **Title:** Horizon record, LINTEL, first lock
- **Source:** the Gate Room's first-lock log, with the duty technician's annotations in brackets

> HORIZON RECORD. DESTINATION: LINTEL. FIRST LOCK.
>
> 00:00 Lock confirmed. Pole camera and sampling line through the horizon.
> 00:30 Grey grit plain. No wind. Sky black. [Nothing moving. Nothing at all.]
> 01:10 Worked stone ahead of the Ring, range about 30 m. Standing disc, about 1.5 m across,
> set on edge to face the Ring. Seven recesses around the rim, each with a carving cut upright.
> Frame 1.
> [The carving at the bottom of the disc is the climbing mark, as on the Gallow lintel.]
> 02:40 One recess empty: lower left. Loose fragment on the ground below it. Long lens. Frame 2.
> [Fragment is upside down as it lies. Its outline matches the empty recess.]
> 04:00 No other worked stone in view.
> 06:00 Sampling line withdrawn. Readings to the world dossier.
> 06:30 Pole camera withdrawn.

### Solution

1. Folder 2 says to look near the far Ring for a stone with seven marks and how to read it:
   begin with the climbing mark, go the way it climbs, and look beneath it if it is broken.
2. On frame 1 the climbing mark is at the bottom of the disc. Its notches climb toward the
   right, so from the bottom the next carving is the one at lower right: the reading runs
   anticlockwise.
3. Going round anticlockwise: Stairs, Doorway, Cactus, Ladder, Butterfly, Wedge, then the empty
   recess.
4. The empty recess is the seventh seal. Frame 2 shows the fallen piece; turned the right way up
   it is the Candle.
5. The address is Stairs, Doorway, Cactus, Ladder, Butterfly, Wedge, Candle. The charge is
   366.6 MWh. The player dials and locks.

Expected time: four to seven minutes.

The likeliest wrong answer is the clockwise reading: Stairs, Candle, Wedge, Butterfly, Ladder,
Cactus, Doorway. No world can have that address (see "Decoys for the validator"), so it always
gives No gate and costs 36.7 MWh. The hint for that spot is offered as soon as it fails.

### Where players get stuck, and the analyst's hints

| Stuck on | Gentle hint | Direct hint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Not connecting the letter and the frames | "Dr. Ellery asked for someone to look near the far Ring for worked stone. The horizon camera did." | "Frame 1 is the stone Ellery hoped for. Read it by her rule." |
| Where to start on the disc | "Folder 2: begin with the climbing mark." | "Start at the bottom of the disc, with the Stairs." |
| Reading clockwise (or after a No gate from it) | "Ellery: go the way it climbs. At the bottom of the disc, which neighbour does the climbing mark climb toward?" | "It climbs to the right, toward the lower right carving. Read the disc anticlockwise." |
| The empty recess | "Ellery: if the stone is broken, look on the ground beneath it." | "Frame 2 is the missing carving, upside down. Turn it over: a narrow block with a short line rising from its top to a dot, the Candle." |

### Answer

**Seals 1 to 7:** Stairs, Doorway, Cactus, Ladder, Butterfly, Wedge, Candle (glyphs 35, 22,
19, 3, 24, 29, 34). Seal 8: CAIRN's mark.

**World: MILESTONE.** A world of blue-white ice where the Ring sits at the bottom of a cut, and
where someone once waited a very long time. **Safe:** to dial and observe, yes; for people, only
in cold-weather equipment.

**World dossier (arrives on MILESTONE's first lock):**

> WORLD DOSSIER: MILESTONE (PRELIMINARY)
> Source of address: worked stone on LINTEL (horizon record, frames 1 and 2).
> Compiled from the horizon record of the first lock.
> Status: Safe to observe. Hostile to people without cold-weather equipment.
>
> The far Ring stands at the bottom of a cut in blue-white ice, open to a pale sky. The sampling
> line drew nitrogen and oxygen, thin but breathable for short periods. Temperature minus

## Puzzle 4: The Night Watchman's Song

**Delivered:** on MILESTONE's first lock, Folder 3 of the Object 58 file arrives in the Archive,
as the Folder 2 release slip promised. It holds documents 4A and 4B.

**Teaches:** a clue can be a text whose meaning is not on its surface; two sources can disagree,
and the Ring can settle what reading cannot (puzzle rule 5).

### Document 4A: Field Note 31

- **Kind:** note
- **Title:** Gallow Survey, Field Note 31
- **Source:** Dr. A. Ellery, handwritten; the first page of Object 58, Folder 3

> GALLOW SURVEY. FIELD NOTE 31.
> 22 June 1958. A. Ellery.
>
> Walter Haskett, who keeps the night watch at the works, has been singing in his sleep since
> the lintel came up. His wife came to me rather than to the doctor. He does not know the song
> awake and says he never learned it.
>
> I sat up with him on two nights and wrote down what he sang. It is a counting song: seven
> verses, each a thing to do. Between every verse come the same two lines, which he does not
> count. He counts the verses on his fingers as he sings, and the first is always the climbing
> mark.
>
> On the second night two of the verses changed places. I asked him in the morning which order
> was right. He said both were, and the water would say. I take that as nothing.
>
> Some of his words are mine. I have not shown him my notes, and Haskett is not a man who
> listens at doors.
>
> I have set the two nights side by side. The verses are his; the spelling is mine. Let the
> record show that I do not believe in dreams. Let it also show that Haskett has never seen the
> lintel's face.

### Document 4B: The two nights

- **Kind:** log
- **Title:** Haskett transcript, 19 and 21 June 1958
- **Source:** Dr. A. Ellery, handwritten in two columns on lined paper. "R" marks the two

> and the far side keeps the time.*

> | First night, 19 June | Second night, 21 June |

> | Up the cut steps, one and one. (R) | Up the cut steps, one and one. (R) |
> | Run the pennant up the pole. (R) | Run the pennant up the pole. (R) |
> | Let the moth go from the lamp. (R) | Let the moth go from the lamp. (R) |
> | Stand like the thorn in the dry, both arms high. (R) | Stand like the thorn in the dry, both arms high. (R) |

**How it looks:** two columns of looping ink on lined paper, the first night's slightly
untidier (written in the dark). The refrain at the top is underlined twice. The pencilled
margin note is smaller and greyer than the ink.

### Solution

1. Field Note 31 says the song has seven verses, each a thing to do, and that the two lines
   between them are not counted. The refrain's bell is not a verse.
2. Each verse names an action, and the thing acted on is a glyph:
   - "Up the cut steps": the Stairs.
   - "Run the pennant up the pole": the Flag.
   - "Let the moth go from the lamp": the Butterfly; the lamp is only where the moth was.
   - "Stand like the thorn in the dry, both arms high": the Cactus (Field Note 14's
     "surrendering man" or "plant of dry country").
   - "Knock the wedge in under the door": the Wedge; the door is only where it goes.
   - "Lie down in the shade of the branching wood": the Tree.
   - "Drop the hook where the water lies still, and stay": the Anchor (Field Note 14's "what
     holds a ship in place").
3. Both nights agree on verses 1 to 4 and 7. They disagree on 5 and 6, and Ellery could not say
   which is right. Haskett's answer, "the water would say", tells the player to let the Ring
   decide.
4. Second night: Stairs, Flag, Butterfly, Cactus, Wedge, Tree, Anchor. This locks.
5. First night: Stairs, Flag, Butterfly, Cactus, Tree, Wedge, Anchor. No world can have this
   address (see "Decoys for the validator"), so it gives No gate and costs 36.7 MWh.

The player may dial the nights in either order, so settling it costs at most one No gate.
Expected time: six to twelve minutes, including at most two charges.
